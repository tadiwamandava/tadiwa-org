## The whole lifecycle in one object

Schematic → layout → fabrication → assembly → firmware → daily use. It front-loads
every skill the later hardware projects assume, and it is the most demonstrable
object in the portfolio: career-fair conversations start themselves when you are
typing on a board you made.

## The parts that are actually engineering

- **The diode matrix.** Understanding *why* per-key diodes prevent ghosting,
  being able to draw the current path of a three-key ghost, rather than copying
  a reference schematic.
- **Debounce measured, not guessed.** Capture real switch bounce on a logic
  analyzer; it is usually 100–800 µs. Set the window from the capture and put the
  capture in the README, rather than copying someone's 5 ms.
- **Bring-up discipline.** Visual inspection under a microscope, continuity-beep
  the rails, current-limited first power at 100 mA watching the draw, and only
  then USB. This is the same methodology as silicon validation, at hobby scale.

## Rev A will have a mistake

They always do: a swapped footprint, a mirrored connector, a missing pull-up.
The industry word for the fix is "respin." The writeup will have a section on
what rev B corrected, because that section is a feature and not a confession.
