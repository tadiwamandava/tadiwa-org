## Startup and drivers

I wrote the linker script, vector table, and reset handler for the STM32F446.
The handler copies `.data`, zeroes `.bss`, and configures the clock tree.
Setting flash wait states before raising the clock frequency is one of the
ordering details that matters at startup.

The register-level drivers cover GPIO, UART with ring buffers, SysTick, I²C,
and SPI. Logic-analyser captures let me compare bus activity with the expected
transaction, including the start, address, ACK, and data phases of I²C.

## Scheduling

The kernel uses task control blocks, per-task stacks, and PendSV context
switching. SysTick drives time slicing, while a priority scheduler chooses
the next task. A blocked task yields the CPU. Mutexes use priority inheritance
to handle priority inversion.

## Proof on wires

Each task toggles a distinct GPIO so I can inspect scheduling on the logic
analyser. `DWT->CYCCNT` measures context-switch cost in cycles. Fault registers
such as CFSR and HFSR help narrow down stack-frame and exception-return errors
in GDB.
